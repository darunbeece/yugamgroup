Secure Cloud Migration

Defense Contractor Success

Challenge: A mid-sized defense contractor needed to migrate legacy on-premise workloads to the cloud while meeting strict IL4/IL5 security requirements.

Solution: We architected a secure landing zone in Azure Government, implemented Entra ID GCC High for identity management, and established a hub-and-spoke network topology with firewall inspection.

Key Results:

  • 100% Compliance with DFARS 7012
  • 40% Reduction in infrastructure costs
  • Zero downtime during migration

Use Cases in Today's Market

Two problems our clients are facing right now, and how we approach them.

Securing the Dark Factory

Discrete Manufacturing 2026 Use Case

The market: Lights-out plants, where robots and machines run with little or no staff on the floor, are already in operation at automation leaders like FANUC, Tesla and Philips. At the same time, manufacturing was again the most targeted industry for ransomware in Q2 2026, a position it has held for several years.

The challenge: A manufacturer moving to unattended shifts connects PLCs, robots, vision systems and MES to cloud analytics. With nobody on site overnight, a compromised controller or ransomware on the plant network can stop production for hours before anyone notices.

Our approach: We separate OT from IT using ISA/IEC 62443 zones and conduits, run edge workloads on Kubernetes with Azure IoT Operations or AWS IoT, and replace standing vendor VPNs with zero trust remote access. OT-aware anomaly detection feeds a 24/7 SOC with automated safe-stop playbooks, and PLC configs and golden images are backed up immutably.

What We Deliver:

  • Unattended shifts with automated threat detection and response
  • Vendor and remote access with no always-on VPN tunnels
  • A tested recovery plan with a defined recovery time for every line

DevSecOps for the EU Cyber Resilience Act

SaaS & Connected Products 2026 Use Case

The market: Since September 11, 2026, makers of software and connected products sold in the EU must report actively exploited vulnerabilities, with an early warning within 24 hours and details within 72. Machine-readable SBOMs become mandatory in December 2027.

The challenge: A software vendor ships weekly, pulls in hundreds of open source packages, and now writes a growing share of code with AI assistants. When a new zero-day lands, nobody can say within 24 hours which releases and customers are affected.

Our approach: We generate a CycloneDX or SPDX SBOM on every build, sign artifacts and record provenance with Sigstore and SLSA, and enforce policy-as-code gates in GitHub Actions or Azure DevOps. SAST, secret, dependency and container scanning cover AI-generated code too, and an incident runbook maps each step to the 24-hour, 72-hour and 14-day reporting clock.

What We Deliver:

  • "Are we affected?" answered in minutes, not days
  • Only signed, verified images reach production clusters
  • Release evidence ready for auditors and enterprise customers
